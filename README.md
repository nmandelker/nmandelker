# Nir Mandelker

Computational astrophysicist moving into industry research. Seattle area.

Fourteen years building analytical and numerical models for complex systems, 
designing simulation pipelines and analysis tools for terabyte-scale scientific 
data. Assistant Professor at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem and currently 
Visiting Scholar at UW Seattle; before that, postdoctoral fellowships at Yale, 
the Heidelberg Institute for Theoretical Studies, and KITP/UC Santa Barbara.

### [scientific-computing-portfolio](https://github.com/nmandelker/scientific-computing-portfolio)

Six projects, written for technical readers with no astrophysics background:

- **Giant clumps in young galaxies** (three linked projects). Detection,
  tracking, and classification of transient structures in ~100 TB of
  adaptive-resolution simulation data, built from scratch in Fortran 90; an
  analytic model of how those structures live and die, tested against
  high-resolution simulations and used to interpret observational data; and a
  decomposition of galactic turbulence explaining why these structures form
  where classical theory says they should not. The catalogs later served as
  training data for neural-network studies of Hubble Space Telescope images.
- **Cold streams feeding early galaxies.** A multi-year and multi-stage 
  international collaboration I spearheaded developing a theory for the 
  stability and evolution of cold-streams in galactic halos. Analytic theory 
  developed step-by-step, tested against simulations I designed and ran at every 
  stage, and forward-modelled into observable signals that independent teams then
  measured, including a collaboration of mine whose results were published in *Science*.
- **Cosmic web refinement.** Novel refinement methods for resolving low-density 
  intergalactic gas in a moving-mesh simulation code, that every previous simulation left 
  under-resolved, developed in collaboration with the code's author. 
- **Prompt framing in a language model.** Self-directed work outside my
  research: studying where and how inside a transformer a rephrased question changes 
  the answer.

Each project README explains the design decisions behind the code and how the
results were checked.
